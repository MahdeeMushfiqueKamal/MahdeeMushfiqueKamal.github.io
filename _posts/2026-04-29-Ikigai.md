I had learned the concept of Ikigai (reason for being) from the internet a while ago -- however couldn't make the time to read the book cover-to-cover. 
That being said, I finished the book this week. 
I started with the hope that this book would provide me a framework for achieving Ikigai but I got disappointed when I realized that this book is about tips for a longer (and happy life). 
Being 25, finding the purpose of my life is more important than caring about healthy practices for being a centenerian.
Here is the short summary of my takes on the book.  

Chapter 1 summerizes the habits, practices and mindset of hundreds of centenerians to uncover the reason for their longer life, which can be attributed to 
1. Ikigai -- of course, 
2. Not retiring early, 
3. Hara hachi -- fill your belly to 80%
4. Moai -- informal group of people with common interest. 

Chapter 2 is about tips for anti-aging, it advocates for reducing stress, keeping an active mind (by dealing with new situation, learning something new every day, playing games, interacting with others), sleep and movement (not being sedantary, walking daily). 

Chapter 3 provides an overview on Psychoanalysis, Logotharapy and Morita tharapy. The authors argues that both logotharapy and morita-tharapy preaches the concept of Ikigai. 

Chapter 4 presents an overview of the flow state -- a term coined by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi (pronounced as CHEEK-sent-me-high-yee) -- an optimal state of consciousness where you feel and perform at your best, characterized by intense concentration, total absorption in a task.
In order to achieve flow state author suggests selecting tasks that are slighly beyond comfort zone, avoiding multi-tasking, being distraction-free for achiving state-of-flow. 
The japanease people thrive for sophistication and attention to detail. 
Even the most mundane task puts them in microflow. 
The author also put emphesis on meditation -- not to achieve Nirvana -- but to gracefully deal with more than sixty thousands thoughts that we have everyday. 
In the final part of the chapter, the author provides us a actionable takeaway. Observe the works that put us to flow mode -- we should explore works of similar nature to expand the list of tasks that puts us in flow state. The subset of this work will indeed be our Ikigai.

Chapter 5 presents highlights from the interview of five super-centenerians and Chapter 6 highlights habits of older people in Okinawa observed from by the authors. The key points are consistant. Eat healthy, live stress free, sleep well, intaract with people, keep working on things which is your Ikigai. What I found surprising was the fact that some people mentioned 'growing plants for their eating' or 'intaracting with their social group' as their Ikigai. 

Chapter 7 delves into the eating habit of the people of Okinawa. It mentions fifteen highly enriched anti-oxident, green tea and Shikuwasa. The chapter also repeats the concept of 'Hara hachi'. 

Chapter 8 is about movement. I was surprised after knowing how 90% of metabolism slows down while we are sedantary. This chapter then presents characterization of different categories of 'Yoga', 'Tai Chi' and Qigong'.
